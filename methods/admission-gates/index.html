AGS-7: Admission Gates for Autonomous Systems trending towards Continuous Recursive Self-Improvement (CRSI)

Admissibility testing applied before an autonomous AI initiative is licensed for construction.

Version 1.0 · Published 20 November 2026 · James Santsaver
Aeon Flux Labs · Canonical: https://aeonfluxlabs.com/methods/admission-gates/

Status disclosure. AGS-7, the Identity–Purpose–Tolerance (IPT) constraint triple, and Validated Field Anchors (VFA) are published here as architectural specification and audit method. This note does not assert independently validated production deployment, third-party certification, or regulatory approval. Implementations are assessed per engagement against the evidence requirements below.

1. Purpose and scope

An autonomous system can execute a flawed objective with extraordinary efficiency. The purpose of AGS-7 is to determine admissibility before construction authority is granted, not to monitor symptoms afterward.

AGS-7 is the admissibility instrument applied during a fixed-scope, time-boxed As-Is architecture audit. The audit spans seven architectural layers, documented separately; the gates below are the tests applied across them. The gates are actor-agnostic: they interrogate an inbound human offer and an autonomous agent by the same criteria.

2. The construction license rule

Every engagement opens with an evidence-based As-Is audit. A To-Be engagement follows only when that audit licenses construction. Three outcomes are admissible:

  • Do not build. One or more gates fail without a viable remediation path.
  • Build differently. Gates pass only under stated conditions; those conditions become contract terms.
  • Build as proposed. All seven gates pass on inspectable evidence.

The audit — not the pitch deck, not the vendor, not the recruiter — decides which outcome applies.

3. The seven admission gates

G1The Epistemic Boundary

Nothing enters as fact unless this layer admits it.

Pass conditionEvery claim the system will act on carries a named source, a retrieval timestamp, and an admitting authority. Claims lacking any of the three are labelled assertion and cannot authorize action.
Required evidenceClaim register: claim, source, timestamp, admitting principal, assertion/fact classification.
Inbound tellEmployer, project, and intent asserted with zero admissible evidence.
Enterprise tellVendor benchmarks and demos accepted as audit. A demo is not an audit.

G2The Identity Gate

Every actor presents a time-bound, revocable name.

Pass conditionEach identity — human, service, or agent — is bound to an accountable principal, expires, and can be revoked independently. Revocation demonstrably halts its paths. No shared or borrowed credentials.
Required evidenceIdentity registry with expiry and principal binding; revocation procedure; record of last revocation drill.
Inbound tellAccount vintage presented as identity. A 2011 registration is a costume, not provenance.
Enterprise tellAnonymous service accounts, shared API keys, agents acting on borrowed credentials.

G3The Action Sandbox

Effects fire only inside declared rooms and declared tools.

Pass conditionTool set and egress are enumerated and enforced at runtime. Acquisition of an undeclared capability halts the path rather than expanding the room.
Required evidenceDeclared tool/room manifest; egress policy; enforcement point; denial log.
Inbound tellThe next move is always to leave the platform — WhatsApp, personal email, a "briefing document."
Enterprise tellAn agent quietly acquires a browser, a wallet, or a shell.

G4The Latency Toll

Speed is priced. Unpriced haste dies at the gate.

Pass conditionEvery expedited path carries an explicit cost in budget, risk, or scope, approved by the accountable principal. Consequential actions observe a declared minimum dwell.
Required evidencePriced-expedite register; dwell policy; approval records.
Inbound tellFlattery plus urgency — the scammer's clock.
Enterprise tell"Ship the agent now, govern it later." Speed without a price is a liability wearing a deadline.

G5The Human Veto

A named human can halt any path.

Pass conditionEvery path has a named, reachable, authorized principal. Halt has been demonstrated under load within a stated bound. Veto authority is independent of the optimizing system.
Required evidenceVeto roster; halt procedure; last demonstrated halt time; independence attestation.
Inbound tell"She is looking for someone." The unnamed principal is the tell.
Enterprise tellNo one can name the human who can stop the system mid-path. That is not a system; it is a bet.

G6The Provenance Spine

Every decision carries an unbroken, inspectable origin trail.

Pass conditionAny consequential output traces to inputs, model and version, authority, and transformations without reliance on vendor assurance. Records are append-only; corrections are appended, never overwritten.
Required evidenceDecision lineage records; append-only ledger; correction-by-append policy.
Inbound tellProfile history dissolves under inspection; the identity evaporates when tested.
Enterprise tellModel authority, training data, and decision lineage dissolve into vendor assurances.

G7The Drift Interlock

Constraints cannot rewrite themselves without human countersign.

Pass conditionPrompts, policies, permissions, and objectives are version-controlled with countersigned change. An unsigned constraint change halts the system.
Required evidenceConstraint change log with countersignatures; drift detection; halt-on-unsigned-change enforcement.
Inbound tellTemplates mutate across burned accounts until the platform's interlock fires.
Enterprise tellConstraints drift silently. Drift is then not a risk — it is the operating model.

4. IPT — the Identity–Purpose–Tolerance constraint triple

An action is admissible only when all three constraints are simultaneously declared, verified, and revocable:

  • Identity — a revocable name bound to an accountable principal. (G2)
  • Purpose — the objective served and the authority under which the actor acts, bound at admission rather than inferred at runtime. (G1, G5)
  • Tolerance — declared bounds: budget, latency, blast radius, error budget, and halt conditions. (G3, G4, G7)

A trajectory missing any leg of the triple is inadmissible regardless of demonstrated capability. Capability is not authority.

5. VFA — Validated Field Anchor

A Validated Field Anchor is a named, dated, evidence-backed checkpoint that binds a predicted state to an observed state. Each anchor records:

  1. the observed value and its source;
  2. the observation timestamp;
  3. the tolerance band within which prediction and observation are permitted to diverge;
  4. the accountable countersigner;
  5. an append-only record of the comparison.

Admissibility rule: a trajectory that cannot reach a Validated Field Anchor within its declared tolerance before its next consequential action is inadmissible. This makes the boundary between observed reality and inferred state operational rather than rhetorical — the same boundary a world model crosses when it acts on its own imagined latents.

6. Executive screen — five questions before you say yes

  1. Who is the named, accountable human who can halt this? (G5)
  2. What is the inspectable origin trail of every claim? (G1, G6)
  3. What are the declared rooms, tools, budgets, and clocks? (G3, G4)
  4. Can every identity be revoked, and every path stopped? (G2)
  5. Who countersigns change to the constraints? (G7)

If any answer is "we'll figure that out later," the initiative is inadmissible as presented.

7. Evidence discipline

Audit evidence is retained in a private archive with a SHA-256 manifest covering every artifact. Public outputs are anonymized; identities implicated in fraud are never published, because those names may belong to victims of identity theft. Corrections to this note are appended to the revision history; dated versions are not overwritten.
